## SCRIPT

**[INTRO]**
The price reduction conversation is one of the things most agents avoid until it's too late. They let the listing sit, they hope the market shifts, they wait for the seller to bring it up first. And by the time they finally have the talk, the listing is stale, the seller is bitter, and the damage is already done.

I'm Ashley Diep. I'm a Houston real estate agent and I coach agents on building a listing-based business. And today I'm going to walk you through exactly how I handle this conversation — when to have it, what to say, and how to frame it so your seller feels like the price reduction was their idea.

---

**[WHY TIMING MATTERS]**
The biggest mistake agents make is waiting too long. You have a 14-day showing window. If you're not getting showings in the first 14 days, the price is the problem. Not the marketing. Not the photos. Not the time of year. The price.

So the rule I follow: if I don't have a showing in the first 7 days, I'm already preparing the price reduction conversation. If I don't have an offer by day 14, I'm having it.

Don't wait for 30 or 45 days to bring it up. By then you've lost all momentum and you're fighting an uphill battle.

---

**[THE SETUP — BEFORE THE CALL]**
Before you call your seller, you need to come prepared with data. Not feelings. Data.

Pull three things:
1. Days on market vs. competing homes — what's selling and what's sitting
2. Price per square foot comparison — where your listing lands vs. what closed last 30 days
3. Showing feedback — if you've had showings, what did buyers say?

When you walk into this conversation with data, you take yourself out of it. You're not the one saying the price is wrong. The market is saying it.

---

**[THE SCRIPT]**
Here's how I open the conversation:

*"Hey [Seller name], I wanted to check in and talk strategy with you. I've been watching the market closely and I want to make sure we're positioned to actually get this sold — not just listed. Can I share what I'm seeing?"*

Then I walk them through the data. I show them what's sold, what's sitting, and where we land. And then I say:

*"Based on what the market is telling us, I think we have two options. We can stay at this price and wait — but the longer we sit, the more buyers are going to wonder what's wrong with the home. Or we can make a strategic price adjustment now, before we lose any more momentum, and create a new wave of urgency. I've seen sellers who adjusted early walk away with more money than sellers who waited. What are your thoughts?"*

Notice I didn't say "you need to drop the price." I said "strategic price adjustment." I gave them two options. I put the ball in their court.

---

**[THE OBJECTION — "Let's just wait"]**
If they say "let's just wait a little longer" — here's what I say:

*"I completely understand and I want to respect that. Can I ask — what would need to happen for you to feel ready to adjust? Is there a date, or a number of showings, or something specific you're waiting on? Let's set a trigger together so we have a plan either way."*

This does two things. It gets them thinking concretely instead of just stalling. And it gets a commitment out of them — even if it's a future one.

---

**[CLOSE]**
The price reduction conversation doesn't have to be the thing you dread. When you have data, a clear script, and you approach it as a strategy conversation — not a failure conversation — your sellers will follow your lead.
