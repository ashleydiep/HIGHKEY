## PART 1 — WHAT IT DOES

### The Daily Brief `[1:30–5:00]`
*[Pull up Telegram and show the morning brief arriving]*

Every morning, before I dial, I get a message on my phone. It's my daily brief. My AI generates it automatically overnight and sends it to me on Telegram.

Here's what's in it:

It shows me my full schedule already time-blocked. It tells me my exact call targets for the morning — who to dial, what neighborhood, based on where my pipeline is weakest. It shows me every seller who's overdue for a follow-up, ranked by how urgent they are. It tells me what content to post that day. And it gives me my numbers — deals closed, appointments set, contacts made — compared to where I need to be to hit my goal by end of year.

I didn't have to open my CRM. I didn't have to build a to-do list. I didn't have to figure out what to focus on. The AI did all of that overnight and handed it to me in one message.

Before I built this, I used to spend the first 30 to 45 minutes of my morning just figuring out what to do. Now I open Telegram, read my brief, and I'm dialing by 8.

That's not a small thing. Over a year, that's probably 150 hours I got back. That's almost a full month of working days.

---

### The Breakfast Club Pipeline `[5:00–8:00]`
*[Screen record — show the breakfast-club inbox folder with the .m4a files]*

The second tool is one that most agents would kill for and don't even know exists.

I train every morning with Chad Cooley on the Breakfast Club. Best sales training I've ever had. Scripts, objection handlers, tonality, role plays — every morning. The problem is — when the call ends, all of that training lives in my memory. And my memory is not perfect.

So I built a pipeline that automatically processes every single Breakfast Club recording.

Here's what happens: Every session gets transcribed. Then it gets analyzed — what was the main topic, what scripts were taught, what objections were handled and what were the exact responses. Then it gets stored in a searchable database.

So now, if I'm about to call someone who told me last month 'I'm waiting for the market to get better' — I can type that into my system and pull up every time Cooley has ever coached on that specific objection. The exact words. The exact response. In seconds.

I don't have to remember. I don't have to rewatch three hours of recordings. I just search.

That is months of elite sales training, instantly searchable, available to me any time I need it.

### The Follow-Up Automation `[8:00–11:30]`
*[Screen record GHL — show a lead contact with no notes, then show the automated text that went out]*

The third thing — and this is the one I'm most proud of right now — is what I call the dead lead revival system.

Here's the situation I was in. I had over 190 leads in my pipeline that hadn't been touched in more than 90 days. A full quarter. Some of them were warm at one point — they had a reason to sell, they had a timeline — and then life happened, I got busy, and they just sat there going cold.

Most agents in that situation do one of two things. They either ignore the problem, or they bulk blast everybody with some generic "just checking in" message that nobody responds to.

I built something different.

Right now I'm going through 50 of these leads every single day. I call every one of them.

Here's where the AI comes in. For every lead I actually reach, I write notes on what they said and add a manual follow-up task. That's the human side.

But for the ones I don't reach — which on any given day is most of them — I don't add any notes. And my system watches for exactly that.

At the end of every day, it scans my CRM and finds every contact I attempted but didn't write notes for. Then it looks at everything it already knows about that lead — their motivation, their timeline, what they told me the last time we spoke — and it writes them a personalized text based on that context. Not a template. An actual message that sounds like it came from me. And it sends it automatically.

*(show example — something like: "Hey [Name], I know we haven't connected in a while — last time we spoke you mentioned you were thinking about timing. Just wanted to check in and see if anything's changed.")*

If they respond, the system reads their reply, updates their notes in the CRM automatically, and sends me a notification on Telegram telling me to respond.

So in a single day I'm having 50 conversations — some live, some on autopilot — and nothing falls through the cracks.

And once I've finished cleaning up every lead in my pipeline and every contact has real notes and context? That's when the next phase kicks in. I'm building an AI follow-up agent that can actually hold full conversations with these leads on its own — qualifying them, handling objections, keeping the relationship warm — without me having to touch it at all. But that's a whole other video.

### The Concept `[14:30–17:00]`

The way I think about it is three layers.

**Layer one is context** — I told the AI everything about my business. My goals, my companies, my daily schedule, how I make decisions, who my ideal client is. This is the most important part and most people skip it. If the AI doesn't know who you are, it gives you generic answers. Mine gives me answers that sound like me because I gave it the full picture.

**Layer two is data** — I connected it to my real business data. My CRM. My Breakfast Club recordings. My pipeline numbers. So when it talks to me, it's not guessing. It's pulling from what's actually happening in my business right now.

**Layer three is function** — that's where the tools live. The daily brief. The follow-up sequences. The transcription pipeline. Everything that actually does something is built on top of those first two layers. And this is where most people try to start — they want the automation before they've built the foundation. That's why it doesn't work for them.
